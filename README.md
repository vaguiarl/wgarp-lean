# WGARP in Lean

This public Lean package provides companion formalizations of finite
revealed-preference results in the retained main text of *A Rationalization of
the Weak Generalized Axiom of Revealed Preference* by Victor H. Aguiar,
Per Hjertstrand, Roberto Serrano, and Özgür
Evren. It also includes Theorem 2 on `k`-acyclicity and the Nakamura number as
an explicit author-requested extension beyond the core retained after the
REStat editorial meeting.

The WGARP mathematical proofs were developed by the paper's authors. This
package provides supplementary machine verification of the statements and
constructions specified below. It also formalizes classical Afriat and Reny
results and a logical-compactness scaling interface, with separate attribution
to their original mathematical authors.

## Checked mathematical scope

Theorem 1 connects the following six conditions:

1. rationalization by a continuous, asymmetric preference function that is
   strictly increasing in its first argument;
2. rationalization by a continuous, coherent finite CMU;
3. rationalization by the coherent, skew-symmetric simplex CMU generated by
   a symmetric matrix of continuous, concave, strictly increasing utilities;
4. WGARP;
5. the pairwise Afriat inequalities; and
6. the pairwise Varian sign inequalities.

Additional checked results include:

- the full finite GARP/Afriat equivalence, including a continuous, concave,
  strictly increasing global utility and positive Afriat multipliers;
- concrete public-theorem applications in `WGARP/Applications.lean`: an
  explicit two-good Afriat certificate yields a regular rationalizer, while
  the same three-observation dataset has a coherent-CMU rationalizer but no
  regular ordinary-utility rationalizer;
- Lemma 1 for finite and compact-parameter CMUs: continuity and strict
  monotonicity in both comparison arguments;
- the asymmetry-necessity lemma, the coherence lemma, and the symmetric-matrix
  CMU lemma, including numerical skew-symmetry via Sion minimax;
- Lemma 5, the compensated law of demand, together with its stated converse
  for strict cross-affordability;
- Lemma 6 for justifiable preferences, both as a relational demand dichotomy
  and as the attained numeric compact-maximum representation; and
- the main-text finite examples: WGARP without GARP, compensated-law failure,
  the average-budget empty-demand calculation, supermajority voting, and the
  coherent but cyclic and incomplete three-moods CMU.

For Theorem 2, a cycle is a list of exactly `k` observations and repetitions
are allowed, matching the manuscript convention. `NakamuraAtLeast Ω n` says
that every collection of fewer than `n` coalitions has a common agent, so it
also covers the conventional infinite Nakamura number. The sufficiency proof
constructs agents from all nonempty observation subsets of cardinality at most
`k`; the converse applies to an arbitrary finite CMU satisfying the stated
threshold. The regression theorem `WGARP.kAcyclic_two_iff_wgarp` verifies that
the case `k = 2` is exactly WGARP. The four-observation example following
Theorem 2 is also checked exactly: it is `3`-acyclic but violates GARP through
the displayed length-four cycle.

## Infinite Afriat and logical compactness

`WGARP.infinite_reny_characterization` in
[WGARP/InfiniteAfriat.lean](WGARP/InfiniteAfriat.lean) proves all three clauses
of [Reny's (2015) theorem](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA12345): for an arbitrary
observation type, GARP is equivalent to rationalizability and to rationalization
by a nondecreasing, quasiconcave utility that rises strictly when **every** good
increases. There must be at least one good; observations may be uncountably
many or empty. Prices are nonnegative and nonzero (free goods are allowed),
and choices and incomes may be zero. The rationalization convention includes
strict utility comparisons for strictly cheaper alternatives. No continuity,
concavity, or unique demand is asserted for arbitrary infinite data.

The proof uses the prescribed-gap architecture of
[Gonczarowski, Kominers, and Shorrer (2025)](https://doi.org/10.3982/TE5878),
Theorem 8. Finite Afriat utilities are normalized by an increasing scalar
transformation with finitely many utility-threshold steps. This retains fixed
positive margins on countably many rational strongly ordered pairs. Closed
constraints then have a common point in the compact product of ordinary real
unit intervals. Density gives global semistrict monotonicity, and budget slack
gives strict affordability. The use of scalar utility thresholds is an
implementation adaptation, not a line-by-line transcription of the published
proof. It requires no nonstandard real representation or limiting continuity.

The supporting modules are:

- `RenyData`: raw admissibility, necessity and finite character of GARP;
- `RenyFinite`: finite Afriat with free goods, zero income and empty data;
- `RenyNormalization`, `RenyWeights`, `RenyStrictness`: bounded normalization,
  positive fixed margins, density and strict-affordability bridges;
- `LogicalCompactness`: compact closed-constraint satisfiability, finite-local
  Boolean compactness and the faithful-description/finite-subset scaling lemma;
- `RenyInfiniteExample`: a checked client with infinitely many distinct choices,
  a free good and an initial zero-income observation.

`WGARP.LogicalCompactness.scaling_of_finite_local_description` formalizes the
scaling interface: a faithful finite-local Boolean description plus the
finite-subset property yields a global solution. This is not a claim that
every economic model admits such a description. The underlying general
compactness is reused from Mathlib, not reproved as a new first-order logic
library. A separate infinite Boolean-chain client checks the generic interface.

## Formalization notes

Following Özgür Evren’s clarification, the accompanying note confirms the
manuscript’s arguments for Lemmas 1(ii), 3, and 6. Lemma 1 uses attained
minima and maxima, with compactness and continuity established in part (i).
The Lean proofs record those witnesses explicitly. The additional numerical
bound `r x y + r y x ≤ 0` supplements the manuscript’s valid asymmetry argument.

## Modeling boundary

For the finite WGARP headline results, bundles are vectors in `ℝ^L`; feasible
bundles are coordinatewise nonnegative, prices are strictly positive, observed bundles are nonzero,
`L ≥ 2`, and observation types in the headline results are finite and
nonempty. The manuscript notation `x > z` means coordinatewise weak dominance
plus inequality. Accordingly, Lean's `StrictDominates` is deliberately not the
stronger product `<` order.

The separate Reny extension uses the weaker raw-data conditions stated above;
it does not change the finite WGARP theorem's assumptions or definitions.

Utilities and preference functions are extended to all of `ℝ^L`, and their
regularity is often proved on that ambient space. This conservatively
strengthens the manuscript's nonnegative consumption-domain statements;
rationalization and demand still quantify only over nonnegative affordable
bundles.

The compact CMU results use explicit compact parameter spaces, which cover the
CMUs needed here without reconstructing the full compact-convergence topology
on `C(X)` or the Hausdorff hyperspace of every abstract coalition family.

The classical global Varian-numbering statement quoted as background in the
manuscript is treated as an external result, rather than a target of this
package. The pairwise Varian certificate appearing inside Theorem 1 remains
part of that theorem's already checked six-way equivalence.

The Appendix A convexity example is not encoded. The former Theorem
3/nonemptiness result, former Section 6 on optimization and duality, and the
separate WARP development are deliberately excluded. The
finite average-budget theorem is an empty-demand counterexample, not the
excluded universal nonemptiness theorem.

## MathEconLib contribution

[Design issue #201](https://github.com/matheconlib/matheconlib/issues/201)
proposes the complete classical Afriat theorem and the full six-way WGARP/CMU
characterization, with shared revealed-preference infrastructure. This
package is the checked downstream source, not an already integrated
MathEconLib contribution. The upstream foundational port awaits maintainer
agreement on architecture and the finite-minimax dependency needed at its
older toolchain pin. The separate stochastic projects and the
`k`-acyclicity/Nakamura extension are outside that initial contribution.

[Design issue #202](https://github.com/matheconlib/matheconlib/issues/202)
separately proposes the checked infinite-Afriat and logical-compactness
extension. The new code is published on
[`codex/infinite-afriat-compactness`](https://github.com/vaguiarl/wgarp-lean/tree/codex/infinite-afriat-compactness).
This is a downstream implementation and an upstream design request, not an
already merged MathEconLib contribution or a claim of compatibility with its
older toolchain.
